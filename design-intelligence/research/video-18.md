## Lesson log

### L18-1 Rebrand your role: you are a strategist/business consultant, not "someone who builds websites"
- Problem [transcript 0:54–1:22]: building sites alone caps earnings: "If you only ever just build websites,
  you're gonna be playing at a very low level, you're not gonna be able to earn a lot of money, and you're
  gonna quickly become a commodity for business owners, rather than being somebody who just throws up pretty
  looking websites."
- Decision: adopt the strategist identity — "Think of yourself as a business consultant. Think about the
  projects that you've got your hands on holistically" — and interrogate each project's goal: "What's the big
  goal with this thing that I'm building? Is it to earn more money for my client? Is it to build a bigger
  audience? Is it to make a website that is really simple to navigate. And then start asking yourself
  questions: Why? Why is this important?" Plus communicate in multiple perspectives so "the people that you're
  working with understand that you're more than just somebody who can turn dials, plug in code, or maybe find a
  really cool color palette."
- Evidence: spoken advice; visuals are talking heads (`t0m55` era). No screen artifact exists for this tip.
- Effect (his framing): differentiation from commodity builders → pricing power and consultative trust.
- Fails: strategy talk without the hard skills to execute it — he immediately gates this: "it's not just enough
  to know that you are more of a business consultant... you actually need the hard skills to put those
  strategies into action" (2:01–2:16).
- Transfer: any maker discipline (web, brand, product) — lead proposals with the business question, not the
  deliverable list.

### L18-2 Practice deliberately and daily — the "eight minutes a day" bar
- Problem [transcript 2:23–2:39]: treating learning as a one-time course/degree stalls growth; skills decay
  while competition rises ("web building platforms become easier... competition is growing", 0:11–0:27).
- Decision: "think of yourself as an athlete in a professional sport. You should see your job as not just doing
  the work, but also figuring out ways to get better and better every single day." He cites Kobe Bryant's 4 AM
  double sessions as the extreme, then sets an achievable bar: "if you just took eight minutes a day and did
  that every single day over the course of a year, you would be better than 90% of the people out there who are
  doing the same thing."
- Evidence: spoken story; sports B-roll at `t2m30` visually carries the athlete metaphor (boxing/athlete
  footage), per protocol not treated as design material.
- Effect: compounding skill from a trivially small daily budget — the step's core promise.
- Transfer: pick a neutral daily trigger (calendar block) for practice; consistency beats intensity in his
  argument.

### L18-3 Grow skills along the grain of your curiosity, adjacent to design
- Problem [transcript 3:36–4:35]: "what types of skills should I be growing?" — undirected upskilling fizzles.
- Decision: choose adjacent skills that excite you — SEO, branding, software/app dev/coding, messaging/
  copywriting — and do a little daily: "if it's more branding, then do a little bit of effort every single day
  to try to work on those branding skills... try to write a little bit every day... try to design a little bit
  every single day." Personal example: "For myself, I really love writing and I really love design, and so I
  try to do at least a little bit of writing every day, and at least a little bit of designing every day."
- Evidence: spoken; presenter frames (`t3m50`, `t4m40`).
- Effect: skill stack compounds in the direction of personal motivation — sustainable by design.
- Fails: growing in "an echo chamber in a vacuum. You need to figure out what other people are doing so you can
  borrow off of their ideas and get even more creative" (4:53–5:07).
- Transfer: pick one adjacent craft per quarter; tie daily reps to it.

### L18-4 Curiosity: scour inspiration sources and learn from other sites
- Problem [transcript 5:07–5:32]: designers plateau because they don't look outward.
- Decision: "My design skills grew exponentially when I went out and started scouring the internet to find
  really cool websites. There's a lot of really great resources to find some awesome web design inspiration."
  He points to his own episode ("38 different sources for web design inspiration" — linked video in
  description, https://youtu.be/llYWhiB6LMU).
- Evidence: transcript-only (same claim as video 17's L17-1 and video 23's L23-11 made operational here); the
  B-roll at that moment is decorative (`t5m05` kinetic caption card).
- Transfer: maintain a curated inspiration feed; treat "looking" as scheduled work.

### L18-5 No one grows alone: lock in a feedback community or a mentor relationship
- Problem [transcript 4:53–5:07, 5:40–6:47]: practicing in isolation means uncalibrated work.
- Decision: "find a community that you can share ideas, that you can also give feedback and really refine your
  skills. Nobody grows all on their own. Everybody grows in community. Everybody grows in a relationship." He
  then plugs his own: "I work for Showit and I run the Showit community, and so if you have a Showit website
  or a Showit subscription, you're automatically added into the designer community. It's a great resource and
  honestly worth the subscription" — while generalizing: "it doesn't necessarily have to be an online
  community. One of my good friends helped me so much when I was first getting started, he was a web developer.
  I'd go over to his house, I'd ask him questions... he'd give me feedback on the websites that I was building."
- Evidence: `t6m20` — screen recording of the "Showit Designer Hub" community site (sidebar: Learn With Showit,
  Video Library, Showit Courses — Showit SEO 101, Showit Animations, Spark '24 Connect, Designer Experience —
  DXP 2024 dates, Private Groups, Events; a post "Making Sense of GA4 the New Google Analytics" with a member
  comment thread, and a "Become an SEO Pro" course card).
- [Disclosure] Self-promotional: the creator is paid by/plugged into Showit, and the description carries Showit
  affiliate links — treat the specific product endorsement as marketing, the general advice (get feedback
  loops) as the lesson.
- Effect: feedback closes the skill loop (practice → external calibration).
- Transfer: any discipline — secure at least one recurring critique relationship (community, peer, mentor).
