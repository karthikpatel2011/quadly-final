   users blank forms… every single [field] is a decision the user has to make before
   anything happens" (0:25–0:34). Cites the Columbia grocery/jam study: 24 jam flavors →
   3% purchase; 6 flavors → 30% (0:45–0:55). "More choices doesn't mean better. It means
   harder." (0:55–0:58). [The study is Iyengar & Lepper 2000; attributed only as
   "a study from Columbia University" in-video — study identity analyst-supplied.]
3. **Smart defaults** [explicit 1:10–1:44] — pre-fill every field with the most common
   choice; button copy can carry evidence ("12 results waiting", 1:06–1:10). Key claim:
   "70 to 90% of users in most products never change the default values. That's not
   laziness. It's trust. When you provide a smart default, users read it as a
   recommendation." (1:12–1:26). Task reframing: from "fill this out from scratch" to
   "scan and adjust what doesn't fit" (1:28–1:34). His stated rule: "Pre-select the most

   momentum." (2:47–2:53). Product proof: LinkedIn's profile-strength meter "is never at
   zero" (2:58–3:04). Rule: "Never start a user at zero. Find something they've already
   done and count it. That artificial head start creates real motivation."

## Techniques / workflows

- **Before/after principle demos**: every principle taught by showing the anti-pattern
  screen, diagnosing the psychology failure, then a repaired screen. Repair vocabulary
  is small and mechanical (pre-fill, pre-stamp, reveal-partially, let-them-build,
  name-the-loss, add-a-reference-number) — good seed vocabulary for an evaluator's
  remediation suggestions.
- **Number-in-copy**: converting abstract reassurance into concrete evidence inside the
  UI ("12 results waiting", "just 2.6%") — a recurring micro-technique (1:06–1:10,
  10:05–10:12).
- **Starting-line engineering**: choose what counts as progress; reclassify user work
  already completed into the measured funnel (2:17, 2:41–2:47).
- **Funnel-stage mapping**: he explicitly scopes principles to stages — onboarding,
  forms, upgrade screens, pricing, signup flows, conversion moments (description) —
  i.e., rules apply per screen-type, not globally.

## Product design lessons (Quadly-relevant)

- Join flow ordering: defer the email+password wall until after the user has done/seen
  something real (R-A4-04/05) — maps to Quadly's onboarding-profiles flow: let users
  see matched ambassadors or a preview before forcing the account, and never blur a
  result they already earned.
- Onboarding meters: any progress UI counts completed actions (profile started =
  20%+); never render 0% (R-A4-03); progress must be truthful (R-A4-10).
- Forms: pre-fill or pre-select wherever a common answer exists (R-A4-01); keep the
  3-field join form as-is — it already passes R-A4-02's spirit; helper/CTA copy should
  carry evidence where the backend knows counts (R-A4-09).
- Upgrade/retention screens: state the real cost of inaction in product terms, without
  fabricated countdowns (R-A4-07 with the ethics gate ON).
- Pricing (future Quadly tiers): show relative framing ("under 1% of X", tier
  comparison) instead of a bare number (R-A4-08), which is legitimate anchoring as long
  as the anchor is real.

## Open questions

- Threshold provenance: the 70–90% defaults figure, 2,000% sample uplift, and "twice"
  loss-aversion coefficient need external verification before any threshold is baked
  into the evaluator (outside this video's scope).
- R-A4-04 vs friction-fatigue tension: at what point does pre-signup investment itself
  become the wall? The video gives no quit-cost analysis.
- Does `spring loss-framing` degrade trust if overused (threat fatigue)? No data here;
  A3/A6 may add context.
- Anchoring ethics: "just 2.6%" reframing is fully accurate yet engineered — is
  accurate-but-engineered framing inside or outside the dark-pattern gate the
  description promises? Needs a policy decision for R-A4-07/08 severity.
- Fake-progress boundary: he resolves "artificial head start" as legitimate when it
  counts real actions; the evaluator needs that distinction hard-coded (done via
  R-A4-03 vs R-A4-10) but enforcement requires reading state, not pixels — a build-time
  check against app state, not a visual audit.
