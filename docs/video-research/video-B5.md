## The 13 rules — timestamped concepts (framework names as he names them)

1. **"Think of churn like a leaky bucket. And then diagnose what's actually happening."**
   [0:31–1:07] — MRR grows only when new > churned (sell 5 / lose 4 = grows; 5/5 = flat).
   Tactic: track joins vs cancels monthly and force a growing/flat/declining label.
2. **"Use retention benchmarks"** [1:10–2:39] — School averages: **80% monthly retention =
   20% churn**; best groups **<10% churn (90% retention)**; "not good" **<70% retention
   (30%+)** [1:42–1:59]. Under 7/10 staying monthly → retention is "the highest ROI lever."
   Churn 20%→10% doesn't add ~10% — it **doubles LTV** [2:11–2:24]: "the smaller the number,
   the bigger the change." Scoped by him to consumer/online services [1:17–1:39].
   *(Mid-video ad [2:41–3:18]: free "10-stage roadmap" lead magnet — commercial context.)*
3. **"The biggest rule is that churn drops dramatically with time."** [3:19–5:10] — credited
   to Sam. Cohorts: **months 1–3 churn 20%+; past day 90 drops to ~10%; by month 6, ~2%**
   (5× LTV swing) [3:50–4:18]. Reframe: "how do I get someone past day 90 / month 6."
   Caveat: businesses <90 days old have structurally inflated churn — "don't overhaul your
   entire business because your month one churn sucks." **Milestone riff** [5:11–5:39]:
   month 1 ← activation, onboarding, expectation setting, selling the right promises, daily
   presence; day 90 ← a **delivered outcome / behavior change**; month 6 ← **connections
   with other members**.
4. **"Ask people why they canceled, and then look for the patterns."** [5:41–6:43] — Sam's
   head of support sits next to him for a "tight feedback loop" between customer problems,
   cancel reasons, and the build queue [5:58–6:12]. Tactic: DM script; tag reasons —
   **price, overwhelm, "not using," missing a feature, "not what I expected"** [6:31–6:37];
   every ~20 cancels, fix the top 1–2.
5. **"Ask your best members why they stay… and then do more of that."** [6:46–7:58] —
   interview longest-tenured/most-engaged; "whatever shows up repeatedly becomes your
   retention engine… everything else is really optional." Fix through "**subtraction and
   amelioration**," not addition [7:22–7:26]; quality = keep the promise, "remove all
   friction" in getting it [7:33–7:39].
6. **"Price objections — you don't want to guess, you want to tier based on evidence."**
   [8:00–9:26] — School added an **11× cheaper tier ($99 → $9)** keeping the high tier
   (extra admins, unique URLs, no competitor sidebars). Rule: **ideal** customers citing
   price → add a tier; **non-ideal** people citing price → "the price is actually acting as
   a good filter — great, bingo, it's working" [9:01–9:25].
7. **"Overwhelm"** [9:29–12:16] — "the number one reason people churn, outside of price…
   it is the churn driver"; "doing less can often retain more." **Planet Fitness** [9:51–11:07]:
   surveyed $59/mo health clubs, most users touched only cardio + a little weights (~10–15%
   of the facility), stripped to that at $10/mo; paying for 5 things while using 1 = "feel
   like they're only getting 20% of the value" — added features give "more reason to cancel."
   A School community **deleted content; churn fell 30% → 5% MoM — a 6× LTV increase**
   [11:07–11:19]. Tactic: "if I deleted everything, what one thing would you fight for? /
   which one feature would you not mind seeing go?" [11:26–11:41]. Principle: "**value per
   second, not seconds of value**" — one valuable thing per week keeps someone [12:02–12:15].
8. **"Design for the extremes."** [12:16–12:54] — a **busy path** (value "from a distance,"
   never feel left behind) *and* power-user depth ("side quests").
9. **"Move the ladder closer to the front"** [12:54–13:43] — **"snakes and ladders"** framing:
   remove snakes (friction, confusion, overwhelm); the long ladder (the sticky thing) "put it
   in the onboarding." **Pin the fast win** — value "ideally within the first 24 hours"
   [13:28–13:31].
10. **"Show up daily and care."** [13:43–15:05] — daily-active owners had "significantly
    higher retention… we've basically never seen this fail." Read complaints every morning;
    praise good behavior; "**delete bad stuff and delete bad people**… deleting cancer."
11. **"A weekly call and a recording."** [15:07–15:53] — "live voice / face deepens
    relationships"; recordings serve busy people; TV weekly-cadence analogy. Default:
    "**one big thing per week** plus a best-of post."
12. **"Add an annual plan with a discount."** [15:54–16:44] — "annual always has lower churn
    than monthly… you only need to decide one time rather than 12"; annual buyers also more
    committed, discounted, qualified upfront. Common practice **~16–17% = "buy 10 get 2."**
    "Stabilizes retention."
13. **"Build belonging via one-on-one relationships and 10 true regulars."** [16:44–18:43] —
    Layla's relayed stat: an employee with one real friend "stays five times longer" — "hack
    of hacks: get them to make one real friend." Introduce regulars to new members (party-host
    matchmaking; avoid in-group/out-group feeling). Digitally: **anchor engagement with 10
    regulars** — top-10 "model citizens": DM, learn goals, spotlight, invite contributions,
    public recognition. **Close** [18:50–19:28]: implement one tactic at a time until
    consistent; "doing 100 small things consistently over a very long period of time"; real
    growth is "**a flat retention curve**."
